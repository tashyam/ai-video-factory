# The hook (first 5 seconds)

## How the reference does it

| Time | Picture | Words |
|---|---|---|
| 0.0 s | Inside the car, the schemer already smirking, foot on the pedal | "He slammed the brakes" |
| 0.8–1.5 s | Camera flies out through the windshield | "on the highway" |
| 1.8–3.4 s | Wide tracking shot: the car behind closes in | "and the car behind him smashes" |
| 3.5–4.1 s | Impact, debris, the rear driver's shocked face | "right into his bumper." |
| 4.7 s | He climbs out holding his neck | the next sentence starts the next question |

The title ("Why Drivers Started Filming The Road") asks a question. The first sentence shows an event that doesn't
seem to answer it. That gap holds the viewer, and the answer is held back to the final 2 seconds.

## Checklist (all must pass)

1. **Frame 1 is a face with intent**: the schemer, sly expression (smug or grit), already moving. No title card,
   logo, "did you know" or establishing shot.
2. **The first word is "He"** (or the schemer's role). The first verb is the misdeed, in the past tense, with force
   ("slammed", "swapped", "dropped").
3. **A visible result before 4.5 s**: an impact, a mess, money, a scream. It lands on the last word of the sentence.
4. **The first sentence doesn't answer the title** and doesn't name the invention.
5. **The motive or loophole is said by 10 s** (sentence 2, with "because").
6. **The camera moves from frame 1**, with a cut or in-world transition before 2 s and another before 5 s.
7. **Exactly one sentence, 15 to 18 words**, ending with a punch word.

## Hook variants (to keep videos from all opening the same way)

All of them start on the schemer in action. Pick the one that fits the scheme.

| Variant | Pattern | Example shape |
|---|---|---|
| **Misdeed and consequence** (reference) | He did X and Y happened to someone | "He slammed the brakes… and the car behind him smashes into his bumper." |
| **Caught mid-act** | Close on the hands, then pull back to show the scale | "He peeled the price tag off the cheap shirt and stuck it on the leather jacket." |
| **Fake-out** | He looks like a victim, then the wink | "He fell flat on the wet floor of the supermarket, and the manager came running." |
| **Instant reward** | The payoff first, the trick in the same breath | "He walked out of the store with a new TV, and he didn't pay a cent." |
| **Innocent setup, sudden turn** | Everyday action, then the twist on the last word | "He ordered the most expensive meal on the menu and then pulled a hair out of his own head." |
| **Repeat count** | Open on the absurd repeat | "It was the third time this month his parcel never arrived, and the third refund." |
