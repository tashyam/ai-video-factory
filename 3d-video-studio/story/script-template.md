# Script template

Fill in the blanks, then rewrite for flow. Target 225 to 245 words (60 to 64 s at 3.8 words/s), 16 to 19 sentences,
one sentence per line in `script.json`. Brackets are notes, not narration.

```
[1 HOOK, 15–18 w]        He ___(forceful misdeed)___, and ___(visible result on someone else)___.
[2 LOOPHOLE, 18–22 w]    He ___(fake-out act)___, because he figures ___(the rule he exploits)___.
[3 PAYOFF, 20–24 w]      And sure enough, ___(who pays)___ pays for ___, ___, and ___(funniest reward last)___.
[4 REPEAT, 10–12 w]      So the next ___(time unit)___, he does it again ___(with a twist)___.
[5 LIST, 4 w + 14–18 w]  He picks them carefully. ___(target 1)___, ___(target 2)___, ___(target 3)___.
[6 ESCALATION 1, 12–16]  Then he brings ___(helper or tool)___ who ___(makes the trick harder to escape)___.
[7 EXCESS, 14–18 w]      The money comes in so fast, he ___(absurd purchase)___, just to ___(repeat the scheme with it)___.
[8 PUSHBACK, 3 lines]    Eventually, ___(the authority)___ starts calling.
                         "Sir, this is your ___(ordinal)___ ___(claim)___ this ___(period)___."     ← spoken line A
                         "Yeah, I've got a really ___(deadpan excuse)___."                      ← spoken line B
[9 BUT #1, 18–22 w]      But it's ___(why nobody can prove it)___, and he's counting on ___(the loophole)___.
[10 QUIET VICTIM, 22–26] Then, one rainy ___(time)___, he picks a quiet ___(person)___ in ___(humble detail)___,
                         ___(does the full trick again)___, and ___(starts the fake-out)___.
[11 BUT #2 + REVEAL]     But the quiet ___ doesn't ___, and he doesn't even ___.
                         He just points at ___(small, humble description of the invention)___.
                         It's a ___(generic name)___, and it ___(shows exactly what really happened, ending on a deadpan word)___.
[12 TAG, 7–9 w]          And that's how the ___(invention)___ was invented.
```

## Wording rules

- Past tense for the hook, then a relaxed mix of past and present like spoken storytelling (the reference switches
  freely: "slammed… smashes").
- Short, everyday words. No idioms that need explaining, no em dashes, no rhetorical questions.
- Every sentence must be easy to **show** in one or two camera setups. If a line can't be pictured, cut it.
- One funny concrete detail per sentence at most; the funniest one goes last in the sentence.
- The invention is described humbly at the reveal ("a little box taped to his windshield"), not with its brand-like name.
